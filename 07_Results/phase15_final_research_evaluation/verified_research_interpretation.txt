FINAL VERIFIED RESEARCH PERFORMANCE INTERPRETATION
==============================================================================

Important reporting rule
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The experiments below solve different tasks and use different evaluation units. Their accuracy values must not be presented as directly interchangeable.

1. Binary QuCNet TI-RADS-derived proxy classification
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Accuracy               : 54.29%
Precision              : 56.14%
Recall                 : 82.05%
F1 score               : 66.67%
Interpretation          : The binary experiment achieved the highest accuracy and strong high-suspicion-proxy recall, but produced many false-positive high-suspicion-proxy predictions.

2. Six-class TI-RADS comparison
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Phase 9 quantum model  : 18.57% accuracy
Phase 10 classical     : 24.29% accuracy
Interpretation          : Under the matched class-weighted TI-RADS comparison, the classical model outperformed the quantum-inspired model.

3. Strict leakage-free ROI and case evaluation
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ROI-level accuracy     : 12.50%
Case-level accuracy    : 11.36%
Interpretation          : The six-class CNN collapsed to a single class under the strict case-separated ROI protocol. This is retained as an honest negative result rather than hidden.

Final scientific conclusion
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The reproduction does not support one universal project accuracy. It supports a task-specific set of results: 54.29% for TI-RADS-derived binary proxy classification, 18.57% versus 24.29% in the matched quantum-versus-classical TI-RADS comparison, and 12.50% ROI / 11.36% case accuracy for the strict leakage-free six-class baseline.
The central research contribution is the discovery that stricter case-level and ROI-level evaluation substantially reduces the optimistic performance observed in earlier image-level experiments.
